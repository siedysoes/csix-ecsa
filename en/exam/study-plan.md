# A Practical Study Plan

A good plan turns a large certification goal into small, repeatable habits. This independent, unofficial guide suggests a routine built on understanding, hands-on practice, and documentation. It is not affiliated with EC-Council; EC-Council, CEH, and ECSA are trademarks of their owners, and we provide no official materials or exam dumps.

## The six parts of your routine

**1. Understand the workflow and ethics.** Learn the phases of an authorized penetration test and the rules around scope and written permission. Read [Penetration Testing Methodology](../fundamentals/pentest-methodology.md) and [Scope, Authorization & Ethics](../fundamentals/scope-and-ethics.md) first.

**2. Build a lab.** Set up your own virtual machines on an isolated network. Practice only on systems you own or have explicit permission to test.

**3. Practise documentation.** After every exercise, write a short report: scope, steps, findings, and remediation ideas. Clear writing is a real skill that employers value.

**4. Review with spaced repetition.** Revisit notes after one day, three days, and one week. Short, repeated reviews beat long cram sessions.

**5. Use legitimate practice exams.** Use official sample questions or reputable publishers. These build familiarity without breaking any rules.

**6. Track weak areas.** Keep a simple list of topics you got wrong and revisit it every week.

EC-Council changes versions and policies over time — verify the current exam version, format, eligibility, fees, retake rules, and renewal/CPD requirements on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) or with an authorized training partner.

## A simple weekly table

| Day | Focus | Time idea |
|-----|-------|-----------|
| Monday | Read one methodology topic; write summary notes | 45 minutes |
| Tuesday | Lab exercise in your isolated VMs; snapshot first | 60 minutes |
| Wednesday | Spaced-repetition review; update weak-area list | 30 minutes |
| Thursday | Write a short lab report | 45 minutes |
| Friday | Legitimate practice questions; log mistakes | 45 minutes |
| Saturday | Longer lab session or cyber-range practice | 90 minutes |
| Sunday | Rest or light review | Optional |

Adapt the times to your schedule. Consistency matters more than intensity. If you can only manage a few hours a week, keep the routine and shrink each session rather than skipping whole days. A plan you can follow beats a perfect plan you abandon.

## Avoid exam dumps and brain dumps

Dumps violate exam policies, do not build real skill, and may be illegal or unethical. They can expose you to malware and give a false sense of readiness. Study the published objectives instead and practise in a safe lab you control.

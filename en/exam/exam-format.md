## Learning objectives

1. Explain why this guide states no version number, duration, question count,
   pass mark, price, or retake rule for ECSA.
2. Place any exam claim on a **source ladder** and assign a confidence label.
3. Locate and read a certification page and blueprint on
   [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) and
   [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/), recording dates.
4. Distinguish a **blueprint** from a prep-site summary, brochure, or marketing
   statistic.
5. Check eligibility and delivery/proctoring without importing rules from
   another credential or another year.
6. Maintain a **verification log** with dates, quotes, source types, and a
   next-review date.
7. Decide whether a legacy credential serves a goal, using written employer
   requirements rather than reputation.

If you cannot do 2, 3, and 6, you can collect facts but cannot say which ones
you may rely on. That is the subject of this article.

## Why verification instead of numbers

A page titled "Exam Format & Versions" is expected to list a version,
duration, question count, pass mark, price, and retake rule. This one does not.

When the ECSA programme was checked against EC-Council's current certification
catalogue on **26 Nov 2026**, it was not listed among active credentials; the
catalogue showed a different, evolving portfolio (for example CEH AI and CPENT
AI), and no current ECSA page could be confirmed. That is a narrow, dated
observation — not a claim that the credential never existed, is worthless, or
was formally retired.

The editorial rule is strict: **no invented version, duration, question count,
pass mark, price, or retake policy.** We will not launder a prep-site number
into a fact, or write plausible figures "as examples" — examples are what
readers remember. Illustrative claims therefore use placeholders such as `[N]`.

Three terms anchor the rest:

- A **claim** is any assertion about the exam. It can be true, false, stale, or
  unverifiable.
- A **source** is where the claim came from; its type decides how much weight
  the claim carries.
- A **blueprint** (exam outline, candidate guide) is the issuer's published
  description of coverage and structure. It has an **effective date** and can
  be superseded.

A **legacy credential** is one whose current status cannot be confirmed as
actively catalogued. That describes our *evidence*, not its value.

| Question | Where the answer can live | What this guide says |
|---|---|---|
| "Which ECSA version is current?" | Live certification page with an effective date | Not confirmed on 26 Nov 2026; verify |
| "How long is the exam?" | Official blueprint | No number stated; find and record it |
| "Question count and pass mark?" | Official blueprint / policy | No number stated; other figures are claims |
| "Cost and retakes?" | Official price list / enrolment terms | No figure or rule stated; date-stamp it |
| "Online proctoring?" | Official delivery page / candidate portal | Verify per current policy |
| "Does my employer accept it?" | Your employer's written requirement | Only your employer can answer |

## The source ladder

Sort every claim by authority before believing it:

| Tier | Source | Good for | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Live pages on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) / [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/) | Current status, official wording, policy links | Pages change; date your look |
| 1b | Issuer's downloadable guide or policy PDF | Detailed objectives, eligibility, delivery | Superseded PDFs circulate; check the printed date |
| 2 | Authorized training partner with a written, dated quote | Course alignment, local logistics | Marketing incentives; unchecked verbal answers |
| 3 | Public frameworks (for example NIST SP 800-115, OWASP) | Understanding the subject matter | Not exam policy; defines no pass mark |
| 4 | Prep sites, blogs, videos, forums, aggregators | Clues to investigate; study ideas | Unverifiable numbers, recycled content, no date |
| 5 | Anecdote: "a friend took it last year" | A prompt to ask a better question | One memory of a different version or delivery |

**Rule one: a lower tier never upgrades a higher tier.** A blog quoting an
official page is still a blog unless you open the page. If you cannot, label
the claim "reported by tier 4, unverified".

**Rule two: vendor numbers are claims, not facts.** Providers publish pass
rates and lab counts. These may be accurate marketing measurements, but they
are not exam policy and not guarantees about you. Record them as "marketing
claim, tier 2, methodology unverified"; never turn a statistic into an
expectation.

Your **employer's requirements** belong to a different category. They are not
exam truth but the truth about *your decision*: a posting or policy can make a
credential relevant regardless of what the issuer publishes. Both facts can
coexist — the issuer defines the credential, the employer defines its value to
you.

## Finding the current certification page

Treat this as a repeatable investigation, not a click. Menu labels change, so
record what you actually did.

1. **Start at the root.** Open [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) and
   find the certification catalogue; this avoids stale inherited deep links.
2. **Search the catalogue.** If ECSA appears, note the URL, page title, and
   date. If not, write "not found in catalogue on `[date]`" — a dated absence
   is a legitimate finding.
3. **Check the candidate portal.** Use
   [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/) for guides, policies, and
   legacy programme pages the marketing site may not display.
4. **Follow policy links, then stop.** Open a referenced PDF once or twice;
   endless link-chasing makes stale documents look current.
5. **Cross-check with an authorized partner in writing.** Ask for the same
   official page. A reseller screenshot stays tier 2/4.
6. **Use archives only for history.** They explain an old blog, not current
   policy.

"Not found" is **absence of evidence**, not **evidence of absence**: the
catalogue may be reorganised or regional. The defensible sentence is "On
26 Nov 2026 I could not confirm a current ECSA listing in the public
catalogue"; the indefensible one is "ECSA is dead".

Save a screenshot of the listing or its absence with the date visible, any
downloaded guide, and exact URLs. A verification you cannot reproduce is a
memory, not a record.

## Reading a blueprint

A blueprint is the issuer's structured description of the exam. Check its
version and effective date before using it.

| Blueprint field | Meaning | Record it as | Common misread |
|---|---|---|---|
| Version / label | Issuer's identifier for this edition | Copy exactly, plus date seen | Assuming a higher number is newer |
| Effective date | When the edition applies | Date + time zone if given | Treating publication date as effective date |
| Domain list | Topics the issuer says are in scope | Verbatim headings | A heading promises one question each |
| Weightings | Published intended emphasis | Percentage as printed | Weights guarantee item counts |
| Item types | Formats used, e.g. scenario or practical | Verbatim description | Every format appears on every sitting |
| Delivery | Test-centre, remote, or other | Exact wording + policy link | Importing another credential's rule |
| Eligibility | Stated prerequisites | Verbatim + source URL | "Recommended" read as "required" |
| Policy references | Retake, refund, identity links | Link list + retrieval date | Policies from a different exam apply |
| Change log | What changed from the last edition | Summarise with dates | Ignoring it and reusing old notes |

**Read the document's own date.** A file named `guide-2026.pdf` can contain an
older edition; the version printed inside wins. If none is printed, note the
reduced authority.

**Separate stated from inferred.** "The blueprint lists secure design" is
fact; "therefore it will be heavily tested" is inference. Prep courses are
tier-4 interpretations even when well made.

**Do not plan from sample items.** They illustrate format, not the real
distribution. Allocate time from domains and weightings. Platform moves and
version transitions can still differ from the blueprint — ask in writing.

## Claim versus source versus confidence

Every row below is a **shape of claim**, not a fact about ECSA. Placeholders
are deliberate: a real number appears only when *you* fill it in from a
recorded source.

| Illustrative claim (placeholder) | Typical tier | Confidence if that is all you have | What would raise it |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Time limit is `[N]` minutes." | Blog / forum, 4 | Low — undated | Verbatim current blueprint on issuer domain |
| "There are `[N]` questions." | Prep FAQ, 4 | Low — often recycled | Official candidate guide with effective date |
| "Pass mark is `[X]`%." | Video / comment, 4–5 | Very low — high-stakes claim | Issuer policy page or written answer |
| "Training is mandatory." | Provider page, 2 | Medium for their course, low for policy | Official eligibility wording |
| "Current version is `[version label]`." | Aggregator, 4 | Low — goes stale fast | Live catalogue + blueprint effective date |
| "Online proctoring is available." | Reseller, 2 | Low–medium — varies by region/date | Official delivery page or candidate portal |
| "Retake allowed after `[N]` days." | Anecdote, 5 | Very low — older policy likely | Issuer retake policy with retrieval date |
| "Exam costs `[amount]`." | Marketing page, 2 | Low — may be bundled or regional | Official price list plus inclusions, dated |
| "Pass rate is `[X]`%." | Vendor statistic, 2 | Claim only — methodology unknown | Not needed; record as marketing |
| "ECSA is not in the catalogue." | Your dated catalogue check, 1 | Moderate for that date | Repeat closer to the decision date |

Use a fixed confidence vocabulary:

- **Verified-on-date** — exact wording on tier 1/1b, with URL and date.
- **Corroborated** — two independent dated tier-1/1b sources agree.
- **Reported-unverified** — tier 2/4; primary source not opened.
- **Stale** — verified once, but superseded or older than your threshold.
- **Anecdote** — single-person account, tier 5; a prompt only.
- **Unresolved** — sources conflict and no authoritative answer arrived.

The label belongs to the claim *when you checked it*; today's verified becomes
tomorrow's stale. Every row needs a date and a next-review date.

## Eligibility and proctoring: check, do not assume

These rules are the most likely to be copied from a different certification.

| Check | Why it matters | Where to verify | If unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stated prerequisites | Determines eligibility | Official eligibility wording | Do not pay; ask in writing |
| Required vs recommended training | Changes cost and timeline | Blueprint / eligibility page, verbatim | Treat as unknown, not free |
| Identity and naming rules | A mismatch can block entry | Candidate portal policies | Resolve before registration |
| Regional availability | Delivery models vary by location | Official delivery page / partner | Ask about your country specifically |
| Delivery mode | Test centre, remote, or other | Official delivery page, dated | Do not plan travel or a room setup |
| Environment and room rules | Remote proctoring has conditions | Candidate handbook / proctoring policy | Request the full policy, not a summary |
| Connectivity and equipment | Practical remote prerequisite | Official technical requirements | Test your own setup early |
| Accommodations | Process and deadlines vary | Official accommodations policy | Contact issuer before booking |
| Rescheduling and cancellation | Affects real cost | Enrolment terms / candidate policy | Get it in writing from issuer |
| Employer sponsorship | May condition your study time | Your employer's written policy | Ask manager / HR explicitly |

Do not treat a screenshot from another exam as evidence; screenshots age
badly and are often mislabelled. **Authorized testing only**: any practice lab
must be yours or covered by written permission. Researching policy authorises
you to test nobody's systems.

## The verification log

A verification log lets your future self audit your past self.

| Date checked | Claim / question | Source (URL or doc + version) | Tier | Exact quote or observation | Confidence | Action | Next review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Date checked | Claim / question | Source (URL or doc + version) | Tier | Exact quote or observation | Confidence | Action | Next review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-26 | Is ECSA in the catalogue? | eccouncil.org catalogue, credentials index | 1 | "not found in catalogue on 2026-11-26" | Verified-on-date (absence) | Treat as legacy pending recheck | Before purchase |
| 2026-11-26 | Current version label | Same catalogue check | 1 | No current ECSA page located | Unresolved | Do not adopt a prep-site version | Before study commitment |
| 2026-11-26 | "Time limit is `[N]` minutes" | Prep FAQ, URL in notes | 4 | Claim present, no date shown | Reported-unverified | Ignore for planning | If blueprint found |
| 2026-11-26 | Does employer require it? | Job posting `[ID]`, line copied | Decision | "requires `[credential]`" or "not mentioned" | Verified-on-date | Ask HR to confirm | Before application deadline |

The last row is not an exam source, but it drives the same decision, so it
belongs in the same log. This prevents the classic error of studying hard for a
credential nobody asked for.

1. **Quote exactly.** Paraphrasing loses qualifiers like "recommended".
2. **Include negative results.** "Not found" is a finding.
3. **Date screenshots**, e.g. `2026-11-26-catalogue.png`.
4. **Set a next-review date** before payment or registration.
5. **Never delete an entry.** Mark it stale; history is your reasoning.

## Deciding whether a legacy credential still serves a goal

The question becomes "does this credential do the job I need?":

**Define the goal precisely.** "Advance my career" cannot be tested.
"Meet the requirement for role X at employer Y" can be.

**Identify accepted evidence.** A contract may name a certification; a posting
may ask for demonstrable experience; a client questionnaire may accept a
portfolio. Get the wording in writing from your employer, recruiter, or
contract owner. "We like ECSA" is a preference, not a requirement.

**Compare without overclaiming.** Use the log to list what the credential
demonstrably was and where gaps remain. Do not claim reciprocity,
grandfathering, or equivalence without official written confirmation — those
are policy claims like any other.

| Goal | Evidence that matters | Does a legacy credential help? | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract names it explicitly | Exact clause and date | Yes, if current and accepted | Ask contract owner to confirm |
| Posting asks for skills, not a name | Work samples, hands-on evidence | Indirectly — study evidence, not a checkbox | Build safe portfolio artefacts |
| Employer reimbursement policy | Written list of eligible credentials | Maybe — depends on the list | Ask HR before spending |
| Personal VAPT learning goal | What you can explain and demonstrate | Yes — knowledge does not expire | Keep studying; treat label as history |
| Want a currently listed credential | Current catalogue and eligibility | Not by itself; maybe a stepping stone | Compare current options; record gap |
| Client security questionnaire | Exact question and accepted evidence | Possibly | Ask client what they accept |

A legacy credential is neither a sunk cost nor a golden ticket: it is evidence
with a date. Its value is set by the receiving party's written requirement,
not forum consensus. When unclear, "unresolved, awaiting written
confirmation" is the correct state — the same as the workflow's `ESCALATE`.

## Three fictional case studies

### Case 1 — The candidate who trusted a blog

**Context.** Maya finds a blog with a detailed ECSA study plan naming a
`[version label]`, a `[N]`-question format, and a `[N]`-hour limit. It has
hundreds of comments and a course discount link. She builds an eight-week plan
and promises her manager certification by a certain date. The post shows no
publication date in its body; the URL contains an older year. She has not
opened [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) at all.

**Constraints.** Limited study time and a fixed budget; her manager's
agreement depends on the timeline.

**Decisions.** Before paying she checks the issuer's catalogue and finds no
current ECSA listing that day; she opens
[cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/) for guides and policies and
saves URLs. She labels the blog "tier 4, reported-unverified, no visible date"
and copies its claims into a log. Her manager confirms the goal is evidence of
VAPT study, not a named credential. She replaces the certification-date
promise with an evidence-based study block and keeps the blog only for ideas.

**What this teaches.** A convincing source can be well written and wrong for
*now*. The fix is not to distrust every blog but to never let a tier-4 claim
become a deadline. Ten minutes of primary-source checking would have saved a
much more costly conversation.

### Case 2 — The candidate comparing two issuers

**Context.** Ravi compares two training providers. Provider A says in chat
that its course prepares for `[version label]` and that official training is
required. Provider B says training is optional and delivery is remote with
`[specific rule]`. Both offer vouchers; neither chat cites a document. One
"policy page" links to a marketplace listing.

**Constraints.** Ravi cannot visit a test centre in his region and must decide
before paying.

**Decisions.** He builds a claim table with tier and confidence: A's
requirement claim becomes "tier 2, verbal, unverified"; B's delivery claim
becomes "tier 2, reported-unverified". He emails both for exact official
pages and dates. A sends a dated screenshot of an old catalogue; B sends a
current [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) link and a candidate-portal
policy URL. Ravi opens the links himself. He records both answers, keeps the
training-requirement claim unresolved because no official wording confirms
it, and chooses B **for transparency**, not price. He does not adopt B's
remote rule until the policy is checked for his region.

**What this teaches.** When sources conflict, the tie-breaker is traceability
to a dated primary document — not eloquence or cost. Choosing the more
transparent provider did not magically resolve every claim; Ravi kept the
unresolved item unresolved.

### Case 3 — The study group disputing a version

**Context.** A six-person group splits over which ECSA version to study. One
camp cites a forum PDF; the other a three-year-old chat screenshot. Each calls
the other misinformed, and one member has already bought materials.

**Constraints.** No official courseware; members in different time zones; a
wrong "win" would fracture the group.

**Decisions.** They adopt a rule: *no version assertion without a source URL
and a date*. In a 30-minute joint session each member opens
[eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) independently and reports what
they see, including "not found"; they repeat on
[cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/) and record effective dates.
The PDF and screenshot become "tier 4, stale". No version is declared; the log
reads "current version unresolved as of `[date]`". They study version-independent
methodology and set a re-check before further purchases. The early buyer is
not shamed — the group helps her reuse what is version-independent.

**What this teaches.** A version dispute is usually a dispute about evidence
standards. "URL plus date" turns an argument into an investigation, and
"unresolved" is a valid honest outcome.

## Practice by section

Answer aloud before opening each key. None requires a number, because the
right answer is usually "find out, then record it".

**Q1.** A search snippet quotes an ECSA exam format, but you cannot find the
page on the issuer's domain. How do you label it?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

Tier 4, reported-unverified. A snippet is not a primary source even if the
wording looks official. Open the issuer's site and locate the page, or record
"not found on `[date]`".

</details>

**Q2.** The catalogue does not list ECSA on your check date. What is the most
defensible sentence?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

"On `[date]` I could not confirm a current ECSA listing in the public catalogue
on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/)." Add a re-check date. Do not
upgrade it to "retired" or "invalid".

</details>

**Q3.** A guide is named `guide-2026.pdf`, but the version printed inside is
older with an earlier effective date. Which do you trust?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

The version and effective date printed inside. Record both, the filename, URL,
and retrieval date, and flag the misleading filename so it is not reused as a
date reference.

</details>

**Q4.** A prep course claims exactly `[N]` questions from a weighted domain.
How do you treat that?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

As a tier-4 interpretation. Weights express intended emphasis, not guaranteed
item counts. Plan from domains and weightings; do not treat the count as fact.

</details>

**Q5.** A provider publishes "our students pass at `[X]`%". Does it belong in
your log?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

Yes, as a marketing claim, tier 2, methodology unverified — not an exam fact
and not a promise about you. Record it only if it affects a decision.

</details>

**Q6.** Two tier-4 sources agree on a pass mark. Does agreement make it a fact?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

No. Weak sources can propagate the same recycled error. Corroboration raises
confidence only when sources are independent and traceable to primary evidence.

</details>

**Q7.** A brochure says "no prerequisites"; the official wording says
"training recommended". What is your conclusion?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

"Recommended" is not "required". Record the exact official wording and treat
the brochure as misleading; assume neither a mandatory course nor a free route
until eligibility is confirmed for your case.

</details>

**Q8.** A friend says remote proctoring worked for a different certification.
Can you plan your exam day around it?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

No. That is a tier-5 anecdote about another programme. Check current delivery
and proctoring policy for the credential you are pursuing, for your region, on
the official site or candidate portal.

</details>

**Q9.** You check a page, find nothing useful, and record nothing. What went
wrong?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

You lost evidence of your own check. "Not found", dated and with a URL, tells
your future self the absence was observed rather than assumed. Log negatives.

</details>

**Q10.** An entry marked "verified-on-date" is six months old. What label does
it deserve?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

At least "stale" until re-checked. Verification is tied to its date. Re-check
before payment, registration, or a pivot and add a new row instead of editing
the old one.

</details>

**Q11.** A recruiter says "we like ECSA", but the posting names no credential.
Is that a requirement?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

No — a conversational preference. Ask for the exact posting line or policy in
writing and log it as a decision source, separate from exam facts.

</details>

**Q12.** Your goal is a currently listed certification. Is the legacy
credential worthless?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

No. Its knowledge may transfer and some employers may accept it, but it does
not replace a required current credential. Compare against the official
catalogue and record the gap rather than discarding or overvaluing it.

</details>

**Q13.** Someone offers "guaranteed equivalence" from a legacy credential to
a current one. How do you handle it?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

Treat equivalence, reciprocity, and grandfathering as policy claims requiring
official written confirmation. Label the source, ask the issuer, record the
answer and date, and do not act on a forum's assurance.

</details>

**Q14.** A study group wants to settle the version question by vote. What do
you suggest?

<details><summary>Key</summary>

Replace the vote with an evidence rule: every assertion needs a source URL and
a date. Verify jointly on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) and
[cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/), record the result — including
"unresolved" — and set a re-check date.

</details>

## Common mistakes worth memorising

- **Number-first research:** asking "how many questions?" before "is this
  programme listed, and where is the blueprint?"
- **Snippet sourcing:** treating a search excerpt as a primary source.
- **Promoting a claim:** repeating a prep-site figure until it feels official.
- **Filename dates:** trusting a filename instead of the printed effective date.
- **Either/or thinking:** one negative check proves permanent retirement, or a
  legacy label proves worthlessness.
- **Weight-as-quota:** published weights become guaranteed item counts.
- **Anecdote transfer:** another exam's proctoring rules applied to yours.
- **Invisible checking:** looking something up and recording nothing.
- **Preference as requirement:** treating enthusiasm as written policy.
- **Silent sunk cost:** continuing with purchased materials after evidence
  changed, just to avoid admitting an early purchase.
- **Unbounded escalation:** asking five providers instead of the issuer and
  believing the loudest answer.

If unresolved:             [what question settles it, who must answer]
```

### Employer requirement questions

```
1. Which exact document states the requirement? [link / ID / date]
2. Does it name this credential, or describe skills instead?
3. Is it required, preferred, or merely reimbursable?
4. Is it current for my role and region?
5. Who can confirm the wording in writing? [name / role]
6. By what date must I decide?
```

```

## Glossary

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | An assertion about the exam, true, false, stale, or unverified |
| **Source ladder** | A ranking of sources by authority |
| **Primary source** | The issuer's own page, portal, or published document |
| **Blueprint** | Issuer's published outline of domains, structure, and rules |
| **Effective date** | When a version applies — not when it was uploaded |
| **Confidence label** | A short tag saying how much a claim can be relied on now |
| **Verbatim** | Copied exactly, including "recommended" or "where available" |
| **Corroboration** | Independent agreement between sources able to support the claim |
| **Absence of evidence** | You did not find something; not proof it does not exist |
| **Stale** | Once checked, but superseded or too old to rely on |
| **Legacy credential** | One whose current status is unconfirmed as actively catalogued |
| **Decision source** | Evidence about your goal, not about the exam |
| **Verification log** | Dated record of claims, sources, confidence, and actions |

## References and verification

- **EC-Council main site:** [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/) — check
  the current catalogue, locate an official programme page, follow policy
  links. Labels and contents change; date your look.
- **EC-Council candidate portal:** [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/)
  — candidate guides, blueprints, delivery and proctoring policies, written
  references. Availability may depend on account or region.
- **Your employer's requirements:** written postings, HR policies, contract
  clauses, client questionnaires. Only your employer says what it accepts;
  ask for exact wording, not a conversation.
- **NIST SP 800-115**, *Technical Guide to Information Security Testing and
  Assessment* — general methodology reference for the subject area. Confirm
  current status; it is not exam policy and defines no pass mark.
- **OWASP ASVS and Testing Guide** — web-application references a VAPT
  blueprint may draw on; check the current release before using a checklist.

  with authorized preparation.

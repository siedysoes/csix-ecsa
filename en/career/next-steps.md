# Next Certifications & Skills

ECSA is a milestone, not a finish line. This article compares a few commonly discussed next steps **at a high level only**. Certification names, versions, eligibility rules, exam formats and prices all change. **Check each issuer's official site for current details** before you spend time or money. This is an independent, unofficial study resource, not affiliated with EC-Council; EC-Council, CEH, ECSA and LPT are trademarks of their owners.

## Certification options to research

| Certification (issuer) | High-level focus | Might suit you if… |
|---|---|---|
| LPT (EC-Council) | Described by its issuer as a more practical step in the EC-Council track | You want a more practice-oriented assessment after ECSA |
| OSCP (Offensive Security) | Known for a practical, lab-based exam | You want to prove hands-on offensive skill under time pressure |
| GPEN (GIAC) | Associated with penetration testing knowledge and practice | You want a broadly recognised security certification |
| PenTest+ (CompTIA) | Covers penetration testing and related assessment topics | You want a structured, vendor-neutral starting point |

This table is a **starting point for research**, not a ranking, and not advice that any one option is required. Verify current versions, prerequisites, format and renewal duties on each official page. See also [ECSA vs CEH & Other Certs](../fundamentals/ecsa-vs-ceh.md).

## Adjacent blue-team skills

Offensive knowledge becomes far more valuable when you understand the defence side:

- **Logging:** which events are recorded, where, and for how long.
- **Detection:** how an alert rule is written and tuned to reduce noise.
- **Incident response:** the stages of triage, containment, and recovery.
- **Secure configuration:** how a finding is actually fixed in practice.
- **Networking fundamentals:** how traffic flows, which clarifies both attack and defence.

These skills help you explain remediation, not just identify a problem.

## The T-shaped path

Think "T-shaped": a deep vertical bar of offensive skill (scoping, testing, reporting) and a broad horizontal bar of defensive understanding. For example, after practising a lab on weak access controls, also write down what a defender would monitor and how they would verify a fix. That single habit improves your reports and your teamwork.

## How to decide

1. Write down your goal: a job role, a skill, or simply confidence.
2. Read current job descriptions in your region and note which certifications are actually mentioned — **requirements vary by employer**.
3. Compare the *format* of each option (practical versus theoretical) with how you learn best.
4. Check time, cost and renewal obligations on official sites; ignore second-hand summaries.
5. Pair any certification plan with lab reports from [Building a Safe Portfolio](portfolio.md).

Remember: all practice stays in labs you own or systems with explicit written permission.

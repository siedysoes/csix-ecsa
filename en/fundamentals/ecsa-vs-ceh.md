# ECSA vs CEH & Other Certs

Certifications in offensive security are easier to compare by **focus** than by
prestige. None of the credentials below is "better" in every situation; each
serves a different audience, and every issuer can change its requirements.
This is an independent, unofficial study resource: EC-Council, CEH, ECSA, and
LPT are trademarks of their respective owners, with no affiliation or official
course/exam content here.

> **Authorized testing only.** Comparisons here are conceptual. No credential
> permits testing a system you do not own or lack explicit written permission
> to assess.

## Quick comparison

| Certification | Issuer | General focus | Often interesting to |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEH | EC-Council | Broader ethical-hacking knowledge | Beginners wanting a wide map of the field |
| ECSA | EC-Council | Analysis and the penetration test workflow | Learners moving from theory toward structured tests |
| LPT | EC-Council | More advanced practical credential in the same family | Learners seeking a hands-on step after earlier study |
| OSCP | Offensive Security | Lab-heavy practical examination | People who learn by doing extended hands-on labs |
| GPEN | GIAC | Penetration testing credential | Learners wanting a vendor-different perspective |
| PenTest+ | CompTIA | Penetration testing fundamentals | Learners who want a fundamentals-first entry point |

These are broad industry descriptions, not an official ranking or a statement
of prerequisites. Check each issuer's current documentation before planning a
budget or a study timeline.

## Background worth knowing

EC-Council is a US for-profit cybersecurity training and certification company
founded in 2001 by Jay Bavisi and headquartered in Albuquerque, New Mexico. It
created CEH, introduced in 2003, along with CHFI and the ECSA/LPT family. Its
other programs include CND and CCISO. CEH therefore tends to be the better-known
name, while ECSA is commonly positioned as the more analysis- and
workflow-oriented step for people interested in penetration testing.

## How to choose

Ask what you actually want to practice:

- **Breadth of concepts:** a broad ethical-hacking foundation may come first,
  and ECSA-style study can then add structure.
- **Hands-on confidence:** lab-heavy practical options exist from several
  issuers. A knowledge-based exam and a practical lab exam develop different
  skills, so look beyond the badge.
- **Your current job:** vulnerability management and reporting roles may value
  analysis and communication; a dedicated pentest role may also ask for
  practical lab evidence and safe report writing.
- **Employer requirements:** job postings are more useful than forum debates.
  Read what local employers ask for today, then verify current issuer details.

## What the comparison does not tell you

A certificate does not prove that someone followed the rules of engagement,
respected privacy, or wrote a useful remediation plan. Those habits come from
practice, review, and ethical discipline. Treat every certification as a
learning goal, not as permission to test systems belonging to someone else.

EC-Council periodically updates the ECSA version and exam format; verify the
current details on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/). Details for
other certifications should likewise be checked with their own issuers.
